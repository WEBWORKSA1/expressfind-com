Locksmith Cost: House Lockouts, Rekeying, After-Hours Fees

Locksmith prices for home lockouts, rekeying and new locks, after-hours surcharges and how to avoid locksmith scams.

Low$75
Typical midpoint$140
High$200

Range for a daytime house lockout. Your price depends on your market, the timing of the call and what the pro finds on site.

A basic house lockout during business hours typically costs about $75 to $200, and a skilled locksmith can usually open a standard lock in minutes without damage. Night, weekend and holiday calls commonly add $50 to $150.

The locksmith industry has a well-known scam problem. Fake listings quote a low price on the phone, then drill the lock and charge several hundred dollars on site. Knowing the normal price range is the best protection.

What drives the price

  • Time of day. After-hours and holiday calls carry a surcharge.
  • Lock type. High-security cylinders, electronic and smart locks take longer and cost more.
  • Damage. Drilling or replacing a lock costs far more than picking it.
  • Distance. Rural calls may carry mileage or a larger trip fee.
  • Number of locks. Rekeying several locks in one visit is much cheaper per lock.

Emergency and after-hours pricing

If you are safely somewhere warm, a lockout can often wait for a family member with a spare key or for business hours. If a child, pet or medication is locked inside, or you are out in dangerous weather, treat it as an emergency. If a child is locked in a car, call 911.

Safety first. If anything involves fire, smoke, a gas smell, a downed power line or someone who is hurt, call 911 before you call a contractor.

How to save money

  • Call a family member, roommate or landlord with a spare first.
  • Ask for the total price, including trip fee and after-hours charge, on the phone.
  • Rekey instead of replacing locks when hardware is in good condition.
  • Bundle rekeying of all locks when you move in.
  • Hide a spare in a lockbox or leave one with a trusted neighbor.

Questions to ask before you hire

  1. What is the total price, including trip fee and any after-hours charge?
  2. What is your legal business name and local address?
  3. Are you licensed (in states and provinces that require it)?
  4. Will you try non-destructive entry first?
  5. Will you need to see ID proving I live here? (A reputable locksmith will.)

Red flags

  • A vague business name like 'locksmith services' with no local address
  • A phone quote of $15 to $40 that balloons on arrival
  • Immediately drilling a standard lock without trying to pick it
  • Unmarked vehicle and no ID or invoice
  • Not asking you for proof that you live there

Frequently asked questions

How much does a locksmith charge to unlock a house?
Typically about $75 to $200 during business hours, plus $50 to $150 more at night, on weekends or holidays.
Is rekeying cheaper than changing locks?
Yes. Rekeying usually costs far less per lock than installing new hardware and gives you new keys.
How do I spot a locksmith scam?
Watch for very low phone quotes, no business name or address, unmarked vehicles and drilling a lock without trying non-destructive entry first.
Do locksmiths need a license?
Some U.S. states and Canadian provinces license locksmiths; others do not. Ask, and check reviews and the business address either way.
