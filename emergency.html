Emergency help center

What's happening? Get the right first steps fast.

Pick your emergency for immediate, safety-first steps. Then send one request to reach available local pros.

If anyone is in danger, call 911 now. Smell gas? Leave the building, then call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Downed power line? Stay at least 35 feet away and call your utility.

Water

Burst pipe: do this now

If water is near outlets, cords, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out of the water. Never step into standing water where power may be live. If you cannot safely cut the power, call your electric utility or 911.

  1. Shut off the main water valve
  2. Cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe
  3. Open faucets to drain lines, then call a plumber

Water

Flooded basement: do this now

Do not enter a flooded basement if water could be touching outlets, appliances, extension cords or the electrical panel. Call your utility to disconnect power. If the flooding comes from outside, follow instructions from local emergency officials.

  1. Stay out until power is confirmed off
  2. Find and stop the source if it is a pipe or appliance
  3. Photograph, then start removing water

Heating

No heat: do this now

If you smell gas or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the home immediately and call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Never use an oven, grill, camp stove or generator indoors for heat.

  1. Check the thermostat, furnace switch and breaker
  2. Replace a clogged filter and clear outdoor vent pipes
  3. Keep warm safely; call HVAC if it won't restart

Cooling

AC not cooling: do this now

Extreme heat can be life-threatening. If anyone shows signs of heat illness such as confusion, a very high body temperature or fainting, call 911. Move infants, older adults and people with medical conditions to a cooler place such as a public cooling center.

  1. Check thermostat mode, filter and breakers
  2. Ice on the lines? Turn cooling off and run the fan to thaw
  3. Cool down safely, then call HVAC

Electrical

Power out or sparking: do this now

If you see fire or smoke, or someone has been shocked, call 911. Do not touch a person who is in contact with a live electrical source. If a power line is down, stay at least 35 feet away and call your electric utility.

  1. Check whether neighbors also lost power
  2. Reset GFCI outlets and tripped breakers once
  3. If it trips again, sparks or smells hot, call an electrician

Gas

Smell gas: do this now

Leave now. Get everyone out of the building, leave the door open behind you and call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from a safe distance outside.

  1. Get everyone out immediately
  2. Don't touch switches, phones or flames inside
  3. Call 911 or the gas utility from outside

Roofing

Roof leak: do this now

Do not climb onto a roof during a storm, at night or when it is wet. If a ceiling is sagging with water, keep everyone out of the room below and cut power to that area if you can do so safely.

  1. Catch the water and move belongings
  2. Keep people away from a bulging ceiling
  3. Photograph damage and call a roofer for a tarp

Security

Locked out: do this now

If a child or a vulnerable person is locked inside a hot car, call 911 immediately. If someone locked inside a home is in distress or needs urgent medication, call 911.

  1. Check every door, window and the garage
  2. Call whoever holds a spare key
  3. Call a reputable locksmith and confirm the total price first

Plumbing

Sewer backup: do this now

Sewage contains bacteria and viruses. Keep children and pets away, avoid skin contact and do not touch electrical devices while standing in water. If sewage is near outlets or the panel, call your utility.

  1. Stop using all water and toilets
  2. Keep everyone away from the area
  3. Call a plumber, then a cleanup pro

Storm

Fallen tree: do this now

Assume any downed power line is live. Stay at least 35 feet away, keep others back and call your electric utility or 911. Never touch a tree or branch that is touching a line.

  1. Stay clear of power lines and call the utility
  2. Get everyone out of damaged rooms
  3. Photograph damage and call your insurer before removal

Water

Frozen pipes: do this now

Never thaw pipes with an open flame, torch or kerosene heater; it can start a fire or make the pipe burst. If a pipe has already burst, shut off the main water valve immediately.

  1. Open the faucet and find the frozen section
  2. Warm it gently, working from the faucet back
  3. Can't reach it or it burst? Shut the main and call a plumber

Emergency FAQ

When should I call 911 instead of a contractor?
Call 911 for fire, smoke, someone injured or shocked, a carbon monoxide alarm, or any situation where life is at risk. Call your gas utility or 911 for a gas smell, and your electric utility for downed lines.
How fast will a pro respond?
Availability depends on your area and the time of day. Emergency requests are flagged as urgent so available pros can prioritize them. ExpressFind does not dispatch crews itself.
Is it cheaper to wait until morning?
Often yes. After-hours rates commonly run 1.5× to 2× normal pricing. If you can stop the damage safely, waiting for business hours can save a lot.
Is ExpressFind an emergency service?
No. We publish safety information and route service requests to independent local pros. We are not a substitute for 911, your utility or a licensed professional.
