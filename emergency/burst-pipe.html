Burst Pipe: What to Do in the First 15 Minutes

A burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons an hour. Shut off the water, make electricity safe, protect your home and call the right pro.

Get emergency helpJump to the steps

Safety first. If water is near outlets, cords, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out of the water. Never step into standing water where power may be live. If you cannot safely cut the power, call your electric utility or 911.

Right now, in 3 steps

  1. Shut off the main water valve
  2. Cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and safe
  3. Open faucets to drain lines, then call a plumber

A burst pipe is one of the most expensive things that can happen to a home, and almost all of the damage is caused by time. Every minute the water runs, it soaks further into drywall, insulation, subfloors and cabinets. The single most valuable thing you can do is stop the water now, then deal with everything else.

Burst pipes are most common in cold snaps, when water freezes in pipes along exterior walls, in crawl spaces, attics and unheated garages. They also happen when old supply lines, fittings or valves fail. Either way, the steps below are the same.

The first 15 minutes

  1. Shut off the main water valveThe main shutoff is usually where the water line enters the house: on a basement wall facing the street, near the water heater, in a utility closet, crawl space or garage. In warm climates it may be outside or in a meter box near the curb. A wheel-style valve turns clockwise to close. A lever valve is closed when the handle is perpendicular to the pipe.
  2. Make electricity safeIf water is near outlets, light fixtures or appliances and you can reach the breaker panel without standing in water, switch off the breakers for the affected rooms. If the panel is wet or you would have to walk through water, leave it and call your utility.
  3. Drain the linesOpen the lowest faucets in the house and flush toilets to relieve pressure and empty the pipes. If the water will be off for hours, set a gas water heater to its pilot or vacation setting, or switch off the breaker for an electric one.
  4. Contain the waterUse buckets, towels and a wet/dry vacuum. Move rugs, electronics, documents and furniture out of the wet area. Put foil or wood blocks under furniture legs that must stay on wet carpet.
  5. Document everythingTake photos and video of the damage and the failed pipe before cleanup. Note the time the water started; drying within 24 to 48 hours is what prevents mold.
  6. Call a plumber, then your insurerA plumber stops the leak permanently. If water spread beyond one small area or soaked ceilings, walls or flooring, call a water damage restoration company as well.

Mold clock: your 24–48 hour drying window

EPA guidance is to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. Enter when the water started.

Do

  • Keep the heat on if frozen pipes caused the break
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls
  • Run fans and a dehumidifier once the water is off and power is safe
  • Keep receipts for everything you buy

Don't

  • Use a household vacuum on water
  • Turn the main back on to 'test' before the repair
  • Thaw pipes with an open flame or torch
  • Walk under a ceiling that is sagging with water

What to tell the pro when you call

  • Where the leak is and what the pipe looks like (copper, plastic, galvanized)
  • Whether the main valve is off and holding
  • How long the water ran and which rooms are wet
  • Whether ceilings, floors or the electrical panel are affected
  • If other pipes might be frozen

The next 24–48 hours

  • Dry everything within 24 to 48 hours; open walls if insulation is soaked
  • File the insurance claim with your photos and receipts
  • Check other exterior-wall pipes for freezing
  • Consider leak sensors or an automatic shutoff valve

Frequently asked questions

How fast can a burst pipe flood a house?
A fully burst supply line can release several gallons per minute, which is why shutting off the main valve immediately matters more than anything else.
Does insurance cover a burst pipe?
Sudden and accidental water damage is commonly covered. The cost to repair the pipe itself, and damage from long-term leaks or neglect, often is not.
Can I fix a burst pipe myself?
A temporary repair clamp can hold a small split until a plumber arrives, but permanent repair should be done by a plumber, especially inside walls.
