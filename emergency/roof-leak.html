Roof Leak During a Storm: Protect the Inside First

Water coming through the ceiling? Protect your belongings, handle a bulging ceiling safely and get a roofer to tarp it.

Get emergency helpJump to the steps

Safety first. Do not climb onto a roof during a storm, at night or when it is wet. If a ceiling is sagging with water, keep everyone out of the room below and cut power to that area if you can do so safely.

Right now, in 3 steps

  1. Catch the water and move belongings
  2. Keep people away from a bulging ceiling
  3. Photograph damage and call a roofer for a tarp

Roof leaks get worse the longer water sits in insulation and drywall, but rushing onto a wet roof is how homeowners get hurt. The safe order is: protect the inside, document the damage, then get a pro to tarp the roof when conditions allow.

Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the stain on your ceiling is rarely directly under the hole. Leave the detective work to the roofer.

The first 15 minutes

  1. Contain the waterPut buckets, pots and towels under drips. Lay plastic sheeting over furniture and move electronics, rugs and documents.
  2. Watch the ceilingA ceiling bulging with water can collapse. Keep people out of the room below. A roofer or restoration pro can drain it in a controlled way.
  3. Make electricity safeIf water is near light fixtures or outlets, switch off those circuits at the panel if you can do so without standing in water.
  4. Check the attic if it is safeWith a flashlight, look for wet sheathing and active drips. Only step on joists or boards laid across them, never between joists. Place a bucket on a board under the drip.
  5. Document the damagePhotograph ceilings, walls, attic and any visible roof damage from the ground.
  6. Call a roofer for a tarpAn emergency tarp stops more water from entering until a permanent repair can be made in dry weather.

Mold clock: your 24–48 hour drying window

EPA guidance is to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. Enter when the water started.

Do

  • Clear gutters and downspouts once the storm passes
  • Call your insurer with photos
  • Keep receipts for tarps and supplies

Don't

  • Climb on a wet or icy roof
  • Stand under a sagging ceiling
  • Let wet insulation sit for days
  • Sign a contract with a door-to-door storm crew on the spot

What to tell the pro when you call

  • Where water is coming in and how fast
  • Roof material and age if known
  • Any visible missing shingles or storm damage
  • Whether you need a tarp tonight

The next 24–48 hours

  • Dry or replace wet insulation and drywall within 48 hours
  • Get two or three quotes for the permanent repair
  • Ask for photos of the damage from the roof

Frequently asked questions

Can a roofer fix a leak in the rain?
Usually only a temporary tarp. Permanent repairs need dry conditions.
Why is the leak not under the damaged spot?
Water runs along rafters and sheathing before it drips through, so the source is often several feet away.
Will insurance cover my roof leak?
Sudden storm damage is commonly covered. Leaks from age or wear often are not.
