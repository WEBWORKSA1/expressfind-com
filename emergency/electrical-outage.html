Power Out, Sparking Outlet or Tripping Breaker: What to Do

How to tell a utility outage from a home electrical problem, reset breakers safely, and know when to call an electrician or 911.

Get emergency helpJump to the steps

Safety first. If you see fire or smoke, or someone has been shocked, call 911. Do not touch a person who is in contact with a live electrical source. If a power line is down, stay at least 35 feet away and call your electric utility.

Right now, in 3 steps

  1. Check whether neighbors also lost power
  2. Reset GFCI outlets and tripped breakers once
  3. If it trips again, sparks or smells hot, call an electrician

The first question is whether the problem is yours or the utility's. If the whole street is dark, nothing in your home needs fixing; report the outage and protect your food and electronics. If only part of the house is out, or an outlet sparked, the problem is inside your home.

Electricity is unforgiving. These steps keep you to the safe, simple checks and tell you exactly when to stop and call a licensed electrician.

The first 15 minutes

  1. Look outsideIf neighbors are also dark, report the outage to your utility. Unplug computers and TVs to protect them from surges when power returns.
  2. Protect your foodKeep the refrigerator and freezer closed. A closed refrigerator generally keeps food safe for about four hours; a full freezer for about two days.
  3. Check the breaker panelA tripped breaker usually sits in the middle position. Push it firmly to OFF, then back to ON. Stand to the side of the panel and keep your hands dry.
  4. Reset GFCI outletsOutlets with Test and Reset buttons in kitchens, bathrooms, garages, basements and outdoors can cut power to other outlets downstream. Press Reset.
  5. If a breaker trips againLeave it off, unplug everything on that circuit and try once more. If it still trips, there is a fault; call an electrician.
  6. Sparks, buzzing or burning smellTurn off that circuit at the panel if you can do so safely, stop using the outlet and call an electrician. If there is smoke, get out and call 911.
  7. Using a generatorRun it outdoors only, at least 20 feet from windows, doors and vents, never in a garage. Make sure carbon monoxide alarms are working.

Do

  • Use flashlights, not candles
  • Keep a list of which breaker controls what
  • Report downed lines to the utility

Don't

  • Touch a sparking outlet or anything wet near electricity
  • Replace a breaker with a bigger one
  • Backfeed a generator into your house wiring
  • Keep resetting a breaker that trips instantly

What to tell the pro when you call

  • What happened right before (appliance plugged in, storm, smell)
  • Which rooms or outlets are affected
  • Whether the breaker trips immediately or after a while
  • Age of the panel and home if known

The next 24–48 hours

  • Label your breaker panel
  • Install surge protection for sensitive electronics
  • Have an electrician inspect any outlet that sparked or discolored

Frequently asked questions

Why does my breaker keep tripping?
Usually an overloaded circuit, a faulty appliance, a short circuit or a ground fault. Repeated tripping is a warning sign, not a nuisance.
Is a flickering light an emergency?
One flickering bulb is often just the bulb. Flickering throughout the house can indicate a loose service connection and should be checked by the utility and an electrician.
Can I reset a breaker myself?
Yes, once. If it trips again, stop and call an electrician.
