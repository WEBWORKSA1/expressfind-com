Smell Gas? Leave First, Then Call — Step-by-Step

If you smell gas, get everyone out and call from outside. What not to touch, who to call and what happens next.

Get emergency helpJump to the steps

Safety first. Leave now. Get everyone out of the building, leave the door open behind you and call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line from a safe distance outside.

Right now, in 3 steps

  1. Get everyone out immediately
  2. Don't touch switches, phones or flames inside
  3. Call 911 or the gas utility from outside

Natural gas and propane have no smell on their own; utilities add a sulfur or rotten-egg odor so leaks can be noticed. If you smell it, treat it as an emergency even if the smell is faint. Gas leaks can ignite from a single spark.

This page is short on purpose. The correct response is simple: get out, then call.

The first 15 minutes

  1. Get everyone outPeople and pets first. Leave the door open as you go.
  2. Do not create a sparkDo not flip light switches, unplug anything, use a phone inside, ring a doorbell, open a garage door with the opener, light a lighter or start a car in an attached garage.
  3. Do not search for the leakDo not try to find the source or shut off appliances. Leave the meter to the utility or fire department.
  4. Call from a safe distanceOnce outside and away from the building, call 911 or your gas utility's emergency number. Utilities treat leak reports as a priority, often at no charge.
  5. Stay out until clearedDo not go back inside until the utility or fire department tells you it is safe.
  6. Get the repair doneAfter the leak is located, a licensed gas fitter, plumber or HVAC technician repairs the line or appliance. The utility may lock the meter until the repair is inspected.

Do

  • Warn neighbors if the smell is strong outside
  • Keep the utility's emergency number in your phone
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level

Don't

  • Use anything electrical inside
  • Smoke or use matches
  • Assume a faint smell is harmless
  • Re-enter to grab belongings

What to tell the pro when you call

  • Where the smell is strongest
  • Which appliance you suspect
  • Any tag number left by the utility
  • Whether the meter was locked

The next 24–48 hours

  • Have gas appliances serviced annually
  • Test carbon monoxide alarms monthly
  • Know where your gas meter is (but let professionals operate it)

Frequently asked questions

Is a carbon monoxide alarm the same as a gas leak?
No. Carbon monoxide has no smell and comes from burning fuel. If a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911.
What if I only smell gas near the stove?
A brief whiff when a burner lights can be normal. A persistent smell is not. When in doubt, leave and call.
Who pays to fix a gas leak?
Utilities generally handle leaks on their side of the meter. Lines and appliances inside the home are usually the owner's responsibility.
