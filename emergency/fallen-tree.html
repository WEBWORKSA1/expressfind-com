Tree Fell on Your House or Yard: Safety First, Then Insurance

After a tree falls: power line safety, when to evacuate damaged rooms, documenting for insurance and hiring an insured tree service.

Get emergency helpJump to the steps

Safety first. Assume any downed power line is live. Stay at least 35 feet away, keep others back and call your electric utility or 911. Never touch a tree or branch that is touching a line.

Right now, in 3 steps

  1. Stay clear of power lines and call the utility
  2. Get everyone out of damaged rooms
  3. Photograph damage and call your insurer before removal

Fallen trees cause two kinds of risk: electrical and structural. A tree resting on lines can energize the ground around it. A tree on a roof can shift, and damaged framing can fail. Neither is a do-it-yourself situation.

Once everyone is safe, careful documentation will make the insurance claim go far more smoothly.

The first 15 minutes

  1. Check for power linesIf the tree touches any line, stay far away and call the utility or 911.
  2. Leave damaged roomsIf the tree hit the house, get everyone out of affected rooms. If you smell gas or see major cracks, leave the house and call 911.
  3. Shut off utilities if neededIf pipes or wiring are damaged and you can safely reach the shutoffs, turn off the water main and the breakers for affected areas.
  4. Document everythingPhotograph the tree, the damage, and the scene from several angles before anything is moved.
  5. Call your insurerAsk what is covered and whether they need to see the damage before removal.
  6. Hire insured prosUse an insured tree service and, if the roof is open, a roofer for an emergency tarp.

Do

  • Keep children and pets indoors and away from the area
  • Ask tree crews for proof of liability insurance
  • Keep all receipts

Don't

  • Cut a trunk or branch that is under tension
  • Climb on a damaged roof
  • Pay a large cash deposit to a door-to-door crew
  • Move a tree touching a line

What to tell the pro when you call

  • Whether the tree touches lines, a structure or a vehicle
  • Approximate tree size and access for equipment
  • Whether the roof is open to weather

The next 24–48 hours

  • Get a tarp on any roof openings
  • Have an arborist inspect remaining trees
  • File the claim with photos and invoices

Frequently asked questions

Does insurance cover a tree that fell on my house?
Damage to the house is commonly covered when a tree falls in a storm, along with removal up to policy limits.
Who is responsible if my neighbor's tree falls on my house?
Usually your own policy handles damage to your home; liability can shift if the tree was visibly dead or neglected.
Can I cut up a fallen tree myself?
Trees and limbs under tension can spring with deadly force. Leave large or tangled trees to professionals.
