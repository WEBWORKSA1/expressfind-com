AC Not Cooling: Safe Checks and How to Stay Cool Until Help Arrives

Air conditioner running but not cooling, or not running at all? Quick checks, how to thaw a frozen coil and heat-safety steps.

Get emergency helpJump to the steps

Safety first. Extreme heat can be life-threatening. If anyone shows signs of heat illness such as confusion, a very high body temperature or fainting, call 911. Move infants, older adults and people with medical conditions to a cooler place such as a public cooling center.

Right now, in 3 steps

  1. Check thermostat mode, filter and breakers
  2. Ice on the lines? Turn cooling off and run the fan to thaw
  3. Cool down safely, then call HVAC

When an AC stops cooling on a hot day, the cause is often simple: a thermostat setting, a tripped breaker, a filter so dirty the coil freezes, or a clogged condensate line that trips a safety switch. Checking these first can save a service call or at least make it shorter.

Do not keep running a system that is iced over. Running it frozen can damage the compressor, which is the most expensive part of the system.

The first 15 minutes

  1. Check the thermostatSet it to Cool, lower the setpoint below room temperature and replace batteries.
  2. Check breakers and the outdoor disconnectAC systems often have two breakers in the panel plus a disconnect box near the outdoor unit. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call a technician.
  3. Replace the air filterA clogged filter starves the indoor coil of air and is a leading cause of freezing.
  4. Look for iceFrost on the copper lines or indoor coil means the system has frozen. Switch the thermostat to Off and the fan to On for a few hours to let it thaw before trying again.
  5. Check the condensate drainMany systems have a float switch that shuts cooling off when the drain pan fills. Water in the pan or a tripped switch points to a clogged drain line.
  6. Check the outdoor unitIs the fan spinning? Clear leaves, grass and debris from around it. If the fan is not spinning but you hear humming, turn the system off and call a pro.
  7. Cool the house down safelyClose blinds, run fans, avoid using the oven, drink water and spend the hottest hours in the coolest room or a cooling center.

Do

  • Keep doors and windows closed during the hottest part of the day
  • Check on elderly neighbors
  • Book a spring tune-up next year

Don't

  • Keep running a frozen system
  • Hose down the electrical parts of the outdoor unit
  • Open the electrical panel on the outdoor unit
  • Add refrigerant yourself

What to tell the pro when you call

  • Whether the indoor blower and outdoor fan run
  • Any ice on lines or coil
  • Whether breakers tripped
  • System age and refrigerant type if known

The next 24–48 hours

  • Change filters every one to three months in cooling season
  • Keep two feet of clearance around the outdoor unit
  • Flush the condensate line in spring

Frequently asked questions

Why is my AC running but not cooling?
Common causes are a dirty filter, a frozen coil, a failed capacitor, low refrigerant or a stopped outdoor fan.
How long does it take a frozen AC to thaw?
Often a few hours with the system off and the fan on. Fix the cause (usually airflow) before running it again.
Can I add refrigerant myself?
No. Refrigerant handling requires certification, and low refrigerant means there is a leak that needs repair.
