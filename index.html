Seasonal

Stay ahead of the next emergency

Most emergencies are cheaper to prevent than to fix. Here's the one thing to do this month.

This month at home

January: Watch for frozen pipes. Keep the heat at or above 55°F (13°C) and let faucets drip on the coldest nights.

February: Check the furnace filter, test CO alarms and clear snow from high-efficiency furnace vent pipes.

March: Inspect the roof and gutters for winter damage before spring rains arrive.

April: Test the sump pump and extend downspouts away from the foundation.

May: Book an AC tune-up before the first heat wave fills technicians' calendars.

June: Clear debris from around the outdoor AC unit and flush the condensate drain line.

July: Peak storm and heat season. Know where your main water shutoff and breaker panel are.

August: Have trees near the house checked before storm season peaks.

September: Schedule furnace maintenance and replace filters before heating season.

October: Disconnect garden hoses, shut off exterior faucets and insulate exposed pipes.

November: Test smoke and CO alarms, check space heaters and clean the dryer vent.

December: Keep heat on while traveling and ask someone to check the house during cold snaps.

FAQ

Questions homeowners ask

Is ExpressFind free for homeowners?
Yes. Requesting quotes, reading guides and using every tool is free. We earn money from advertising and from service providers who receive requests.
How does ExpressFind find a pro?
You describe the job once. We route the request to up to three independent local service providers who serve your area and handle that kind of work. They contact you directly with availability and pricing.
Do you vet the pros?
We ask partner pros to confirm licensing where required and liability insurance. Always verify yourself before hiring — our free license lookup tool links to official state and provincial databases.
Where do your cost numbers come from?
Each guide compiles several independent cost surveys and contractor price lists into a rounded range, lists its sources, and is adjusted weekly for inflation using U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data.
Do you serve Canada?
Yes. The request form accepts US ZIP codes and Canadian postal codes. Cost guides are published in USD; Canadian prices vary by province.
